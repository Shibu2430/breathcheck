If you've tried BreathCheck's Stage 2, you've probably wondered why the instructions specifically say "not your maximum." It's a fair question — most people's instinct with a breath-hold is to see how long they can survive without air. That instinct is exactly what this test asks you not to follow.

## It's a control check, not an endurance test

The test — based on a widely used self-check sometimes called a Control Pause — works like this: breathe normally, let a normal exhale happen, then hold. The moment you feel the **first real urge to breathe** — not discomfort, not your limit, just the first genuine signal — you stop and note the time.

That first-urge moment is the actual measurement. It's thought to reflect how sensitive your body currently is to rising carbon dioxide, which is generally a better everyday indicator than raw lung capacity. Two people can have similar lung capacity and still have very different tolerance for that first urge to breathe, often related to how fast or shallow their everyday breathing tends to be.

## Why "don't push it" is the actual instruction

If you hold to your genuine maximum instead of stopping at the first urge, you're measuring something else — willpower and breath-holding tolerance, not the pattern the test is designed to catch. It also turns a simple self-check into something people start trying to "beat," which is exactly the wrong framing for what should be a gentle, once-in-a-while measurement.

## What the numbers roughly mean

As a rough, non-clinical guide: shorter holds (under 10 seconds) often go with faster or more effortful breathing day-to-day, while much longer holds tend to go with slower, steadier resting patterns. These bands are illustrative, not diagnostic — a single reading says very little on its own, and this is a self-guided wellness check, not a medical measurement.

## Safety first

This should always feel gentle. Never hyperventilate beforehand, stay seated, don't do this in water or while driving, and stop immediately if you feel dizzy, faint, or unwell.
