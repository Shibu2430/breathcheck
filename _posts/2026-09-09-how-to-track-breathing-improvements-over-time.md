A single breathing check tells you very little. Your resting rate on any given day can shift with caffeine, sleep, stress, or just the mild self-consciousness of counting your own breaths on purpose. What actually means something is the trend across days and weeks — and that only shows up if you're tracking it.

## Why trends beat single readings

Say your resting rate comes in at 19 breaths a minute today. On its own, that number doesn't say much — it could be a slightly off day, or it could be your genuine baseline. But if you check again over the next two weeks and it's consistently trending down toward 14–15, that's a real, noticeable pattern — far more useful than any single reading, good or bad.

This is exactly why BreathCheck compares each result to your **recent average** rather than just yesterday: one unusual day shouldn't outweigh two weeks of data.

## What's actually worth tracking

- **Resting breathing rate** — the clearest, simplest number to watch over time.
- **Breath-hold control** — tends to move more slowly than resting rate, so don't expect week-to-week swings; look for the trend over a month or more.
- **Consistency** — how stable your numbers are day to day matters almost as much as the numbers themselves. A steady pattern, even a moderate one, is generally a better sign than a wildly inconsistent one.

## A realistic routine

You don't need to check daily for this to work — a few times a week is enough to build a real trend line. What matters more than frequency is checking under similar conditions each time (same rough time of day, sitting rather than right after moving around) so you're comparing like with like.
