Breathing patterns drift slowly, which is exactly why they're easy to miss. There's no single moment where "fine" turns into "not fine" — it just gradually becomes normal. Here are a few patterns worth noticing, without reading too much into any one of them on its own.

## Mostly chest movement, not belly movement

Watch someone breathe (or notice your own reflection) and you can often tell whether their shoulders and upper chest are doing most of the work, or their belly is moving more. Chest-led breathing isn't dangerous, but it's a less efficient pattern that often travels with shoulder and neck tension — especially common after long stretches at a desk.

## A resting rate that runs fast

A commonly cited adult resting range is roughly 12–18 breaths a minute, though this varies by source and by person. If a 60-second count regularly comes in well above that for you, it's not automatically a problem — but it's worth noticing, since faster resting breathing is one of the more common signs of a body that's staying mildly "on" even at rest.

## Frequent sighing

An occasional sigh is completely normal. Sighing that happens constantly through the day — especially during focused work — can be a small sign of held tension or shallow breathing building up without you noticing.

## Holding your breath while concentrating

Ever caught yourself not breathing while typing an email or reading something stressful? It's common enough to have an informal name ("email apnea"), and it's harmless occasionally — but if it's a frequent pattern through your workday, it adds up.

## Mouth breathing at rest

Breathing through the mouth during exercise is normal. Breathing through the mouth while sitting still, especially if you notice a dry mouth or throat, is worth paying attention to.

## None of this is a diagnosis

These are just patterns to notice, not symptoms of anything specific — plenty of people have one or two of these habits and are completely fine.
