---
title: "Why most people breathe wrong without knowing it"
---

Breathing is the one bodily function you can control consciously or let run entirely on autopilot. Most of us leave it on autopilot — and for a lot of people, that autopilot has drifted somewhere unhelpful.

## The three patterns that show up most often

**Overbreathing.** A resting rate of 18-20+ breaths a minute, even sitting still, is far more common than people assume. It doesn't feel dramatic in the moment, but it keeps the nervous system mildly activated all day.

**Shallow, chest-led breathing.** Watch someone's shoulders rise with every inhale and you're watching accessory muscles do a job the diaphragm should be doing. It's less efficient and tends to travel with tension in the neck and upper back.

**Breath-holding under focus.** Ever caught yourself holding your breath while concentrating, or typing an email? It's common enough to have a name — "email apnea." Harmless occasionally, but it adds up across a working day.

## Why this is hard to notice on your own

None of these patterns hurt. There's no obvious signal telling you your breathing has drifted, the way a sore muscle tells you you overdid a workout. That's exactly why a quick, structured check — like a 60-second resting-rate count or a simple breath-hold test — is useful: it turns something invisible into a number you can actually track.

## What to do about it

You don't need a dramatic intervention. A few minutes of paced breathing (four seconds in, four hold, four out, four hold — commonly called box breathing) most days is enough to nudge a fast or shallow pattern back toward baseline. The point isn't to breathe "perfectly" all the time. It's to notice when the pattern has drifted, and have a simple way to reset it.
