Before you start

Why five minutes on your breathing is worth it

It calms you down, fast

Slow, controlled breathing is one of the quickest ways to shift the body out of a stressed state — no app, supplement, or waiting for it to kick in.

It affects your sleep

Breathing patterns and sleep quality are closely linked. A few paced minutes before bed genuinely helps the body wind down.

It affects your whole day

Fast, shallow breathing keeps the body mildly on edge without you noticing. Fixing it is one of the simplest levers for steadier energy and focus.

Your breathing may be different from what you expect.

Fast, shallow, or tangled up in stress patterns that are easy to miss on your own. The check looks at three simple aspects of your breathing: resting rate, breath control, and paced breathing.

Breathing exercises

The test above gives you a score. These are what you actually practice — no scoring, no timer pressure. Pick the one that matches what you need right now:

Best for: a quick reset

Box breathing

Four seconds in, hold, out, hold. A steady, even rhythm that's easy to learn and good before anything stressful.

Best for: settling down

4-7-8 breathing

Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8. A longer exhale that's widely used to settle the body before rest.

Best for: falling asleep

Bedtime wind down

A longer, dimmed session with a soft ambient tone that breathes with you. Pick 3, 5, or 10 minutes and let it run.

Stage 1 of 3

Resting breathing rate

Sit comfortably. Tap the button once for every full breath (in and out counts as one) for 60 seconds.

Why: this gives us a baseline for how quickly you're breathing while relaxed.

Stage 2 of 3

Breath hold control

Breathe normally, then let a normal exhale happen and hold. Stop at the very first urge to breathe — not your maximum. This is a control test, not an endurance test.

Before you hold

  • This is a gentle control check, not an endurance test.
  • Never hyperventilate before holding your breath.
  • Stay seated. Don't do this in water or while driving.
  • Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, faint, or unwell.
  • Don't try to beat a previous time — that's not what this measures.
0.0s

Stop as soon as you feel the first urge to breathe, a swallow reflex, or your throat or belly contract.

Stage 3 of 3

Box breathing

Follow the lungs. Four seconds in, four second hold, four seconds out, four second hold. Four rounds.

Why: this checks how easily you can follow a controlled rhythm — not how deeply you can breathe.
