Why five minutes on your breathing is worth it

Gets you through stressful moments

A short breathing pause can help you slow down and reset during a demanding workday — a rough meeting, a tight deadline, an urgent issue.

Helps you switch off after work

Screen time doesn't come with a natural stopping point. A few minutes of paced breathing helps mark the transition out of work mode.

Keeps you steadier all day

Long periods of focused work can make you less aware of how you're breathing. BreathCheck gives you a simple way to pause and check in.

Guided practice

Breathing exercises

The test above gives you a score. These are what you actually practice — no scoring, no timer pressure. Pick the one that matches what you need right now:

Best for: a quick reset

Box breathing

Four seconds in, hold, out, hold. A steady, even rhythm that's easy to learn and good before anything stressful.

Best for: settling down

4-7-8 breathing

Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8. A longer exhale that's widely used to settle the body before rest.

Best for: a calm reset

Slow, steady breathing

An easy, even rhythm with no holds — just smooth in, smooth out. Good for keeping a good baseline steady.

Best for: after a long stretch at your desk

Diaphragmatic breathing

Breathe naturally at your own pace — notice your belly rise and fall. No need to force it or breathe deeply.

Best for: stress / reset

Extended exhale

A comfortable inhale followed by a slightly longer, relaxed exhale. A simple option for tense moments or after a difficult meeting.

Best for: falling asleep

Bedtime wind down

A longer, dimmed session with a soft ambient tone that breathes with you. Pick 3, 5, or 10 minutes and let it run.

Stage 2 of 3

Breath hold control

Breathe normally, then let a normal exhale happen and hold. Stop at the very first urge to breathe — not your maximum. This is a control test, not an endurance test.

Before you hold

  • This is a gentle control check, not an endurance test.
  • Never hyperventilate before holding your breath.
  • Stay seated. Don't do this in water or while driving.
  • Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, faint, or unwell.
  • Don't try to beat a previous time — that's not what this measures.
