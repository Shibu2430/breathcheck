Most people breathe worse than they think.

Too fast, too shallow, or tangled up in stress patterns nobody ever points out. This test measures three things that predict all three: how you breathe at rest, how well you tolerate a breath hold, and how steady you can keep a paced rhythm.

Guided practice

Breathing exercises

No scoring, no timer pressure. Pick one and follow the lungs — these work any time you want to reset, and one is built for winding down at night.

Box breathing

Four seconds in, hold, out, hold. A steady, even rhythm that's easy to learn and good before anything stressful.

4-7-8 breathing

Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8. A longer exhale that's widely used to settle the body before rest.

Bedtime wind down

A longer, dimmed session with a soft ambient tone that breathes with you. Pick 3, 5, or 10 minutes and let it run.

Stage 2 of 3

Breath hold control

Breathe normally, then let a normal exhale happen and hold. Stop at the very first urge to breathe — not your maximum. This is a control test, not an endurance test.

Stop as soon as you feel the first urge to breathe, a swallow reflex, or your throat or belly contract.
