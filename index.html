Before you start

Why five minutes on your breathing is worth it

It calms you down, fast

Slow, controlled breathing is one of the quickest ways to shift the body out of a stressed state — no app, supplement, or waiting for it to kick in.

It affects your sleep

Breathing patterns and sleep quality are closely linked. A few paced minutes before bed genuinely helps the body wind down.

It affects your whole day

Fast, shallow breathing keeps the body mildly on edge without you noticing. Fixing it is one of the simplest levers for steadier energy and focus.

Most people breathe worse than they think.

Too fast, too shallow, or tangled up in stress patterns nobody ever points out. This test measures three things that predict all three: how you breathe at rest, how well you tolerate a breath hold, and how steady you can keep a paced rhythm.

Guided practice

Breathing exercises

The test above gives you a score. These are what you actually practice — no scoring, no timer pressure. Pick the one that matches what you need right now:

Best for: a quick reset

Box breathing

Four seconds in, hold, out, hold. A steady, even rhythm that's easy to learn and good before anything stressful.

Best for: settling down

4-7-8 breathing

Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8. A longer exhale that's widely used to settle the body before rest.

Best for: falling asleep

Bedtime wind down

A longer, dimmed session with a soft ambient tone that breathes with you. Pick 3, 5, or 10 minutes and let it run.
